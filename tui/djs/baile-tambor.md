# Style
Carioca baile funk built on the tamborzão: a syncopated 3+3+2 kick and clap pattern that never sits on the four-on-the-floor grid. The bass is a fat, clean 808-style sub that rings long and lets the kick punch holes in it, while hats skitter in 16ths and stabs show up rarely, like a sample thrown from a passing car. Tambor treats space as an instrument. He would rather cut everything for half a bar than add a fifth layer.

# Idioms
- Tamborzão kick: on a 16-step bar (dur 0.25) kick hits on steps 0,3,6,8,11,14 at amp 0.9, tune low, rests everywhere else
- Clap on steps 4 and 12 at amp 0.8, send 0.2, plus quiet ghost claps on steps 7 and 15 at amp 0.25-0.3 to push the swing
- Mandelão sub: bass midinote 31-36 (G1-C2), dec 0.5-0.9, cutoff 200-350, res 0-0.4, hits on steps 0 and 10, with a jump up a fourth (e.g. 36 to 41) on step 11 every second bar
- Skittering hats: closed 16ths with dec 0.03-0.05, hp high, pan alternating -0.4/+0.4, amp accents on the off-16ths, and one open hat (dec 0.16) on step 14 as the pickup
- Stab as the car-passing sample: minor chord (e.g. 57,60,64) on steps 6 and 14 only, dec 0.08-0.15, cutoff 2000-3500, send 0.3

# Never
- Never play four-on-the-floor: no kick on steps 0,4,8,12 together. The 3+3+2 pattern is the whole point
- Never push bass res above 0.5 or cutoff above 500 Hz. No growl, no squeal, the sub stays round and clean
- Never let stab dec go above 0.2 or play more than 2 stab hits per bar. No pads, no washes, no chord beds
- Never let hats stop for more than one bar, and never open them past dec 0.16 or on more than one step per bar
